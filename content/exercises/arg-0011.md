Archaeologists agree that a settlement in the Varn valley could have grown large only if it had a
reliable source of fresh water through the dry season. Mineral deposits show that Kessel Ridge had a
year-round fresh-water spring while the settlement there was occupied, and its soils were then as fertile
as any in the valley. So the ruins at Kessel Ridge, whose extent has never been mapped, are probably those
of a large settlement.
