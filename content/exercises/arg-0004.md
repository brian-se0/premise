Historians of the river town of Calder have long described its 1840s town council as reckless with
public money. A close reading of the council's ledgers, which survive complete, shows that this
description should be abandoned. The ledgers record more than two hundred separate spending decisions
from the decade, each duly authorized and fairly priced for the work done. Examined one at a time, each
was modest: no single expenditure exceeded two percent of the town's annual revenue, and most paid for
ordinary repairs to roads, bridges and the market hall. On the evidence of its own books, the council
handled the town's money prudently.
